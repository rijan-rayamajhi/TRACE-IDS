## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 Cross-dataset generalization in NIDS

That ML-NIDS fail on unseen networks is well established. Cantone et al. train four classifiers on four CIC-family benchmarks and find near-perfect same-dataset accuracy but mostly near-chance cross-dataset accuracy, with dataset anomalies blocking generalization (Cantone et al., 2024)<!--ref:cantone2024cross--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->. Layeghy and Portmann find that none of seven models generalizes across four benchmarks, that generalization is strongly asymmetric, and, using Shapley-value (SHAP) analysis, that failures stem from features tied to the label in one dataset but not in others (Layeghy & Portmann, 2023)<!--ref:layeghy2023explainable--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->. D'hooge et al. show that learners trained on CIC-IDS2017 generalize poorly even to the closely related CSE-CIC-IDS2018 (D'hooge et al., 2020)<!--ref:dhooge2020inter--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->, and Apruzzese et al. formalize cross-evaluation as a way to expose risks that single-dataset evaluation hides (Apruzzese et al., 2022)<!--ref:apruzzese2022cross--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->.

Two recent preprints sharpen the picture. Atif and Ali report a zero-shot UNSW→CIC F1 of 0.81 for XGBoost on the NetFlow v2 datasets, but only after a staged removal of TTL, packet-length and TCP-window features; the opposite direction collapses to F1 0.02–0.07 for all models (Atif & Ali, 2026)<!--ref:atif2026threeaxis--><!--anchor:section:4.2-->. Their final feature set was selected using cross-dataset F1 as the arbiter, so target labels informed the choice of features (Atif & Ali, 2026)<!--ref:atif2026threeaxis--><!--anchor:section:3.3-->. Hakim et al. test lightweight IIoT models zero-shot on unseen testbeds, obtaining F1 of 0.09–0.28, and show with SHAP that coarse port buckets act as a shortcut (Hakim et al., 2026)<!--ref:hakim2026crossdomain--><!--anchor:section:5-->. Neither study isolates what the source contributes once target labels are available.

### 2.2 Few-shot and transfer adaptation for NIDS

The closest prior work is TAN-IDS, which maps UNSW-NB15 and NF-CSE-CIC-IDS2018 to a common eight-dimensional flow interface and reports that fine-tuning on 5% labelled target data restores random-forest and XGBoost F1 to about 0.98 after cross-dataset collapse (Ha Thanh, 2026)<!--ref:hathanh2026tanids--><!--anchor:section:Table%2010-->. Fine-tuning is compared only with zero-shot transfer and with training on the full target split; in the sections and tables examined for this study (3.5, 4.2, 5.4, Tables 7–10), no model is trained on the 5% subset alone. The results come from a single run without significance tests, which the author acknowledges (Ha Thanh, 2026)<!--ref:hathanh2026tanids--><!--anchor:section:6.2-->. Hakim et al. likewise compare adapted models only with their zero-shot baseline, and name a model refit on the small target sample alone as a missing condition (Hakim et al., 2026)<!--ref:hakim2026crossdomain--><!--anchor:section:6.3-->. Sarhan et al. instead map NetFlow features to semantic attributes to recognize unseen attack classes zero-shot (Sarhan et al., 2023)<!--ref:sarhan2023zeroshot--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->, and a systematic review of few-shot NIDS finds that most studies use five or fewer shots and the CIC benchmarks, and that missing parameters and code limit reproducibility (Roszeitis et al., 2026)<!--ref:roszeitis2026fewshot--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->.

Two studies do include a target-only baseline. Xu et al. pretrain a multimodal few-shot detector on one CIC dataset and fine-tune it on the other. In their Tables 8–9, source pretraining lowers accuracy on CICIDS2018 at every shot count (by 6.4 percentage points at K = 5) and on CICIDS2017 at K = 5, but raises it by 1.3 points at K = 10 and 15 on CICIDS2017; their headline result, with a different fusion method, reports a 0.1-point gain at K = 5 (Xu et al., 2025)<!--ref:xu2025multimodal--><!--anchor:section:Tables%208-9-->. The comparison uses a near-domain pair, a small test set and no repeated runs. Kim compares few-shot domain adaptation with a target-only baseline on host logs: target-only learning wins in one direction and loses clearly in the other, with five seeds, too few for a two-sided Wilcoxon test to reach p < 0.05 (Kim, 2026)<!--ref:kim2026prototype--><!--anchor:section:4-->. To our knowledge, based on web searches for cross-dataset, few-shot and transfer NIDS work covering 2020–2026 and the reference lists of the closest studies, with every hit verified against Crossref, arXiv or OpenAlex, as of 11 October 2026, no prior NetFlow study isolates the source's contribution with a target-only control under repeated, statistically tested runs. TAN-IDS is the nearest flow-based recovery study, and we add that control; Xu et al. is the nearest study with a target-only control, and we add 12 dataset pairs, NetFlow v3 data and repeated runs with paired tests.

### 2.3 Dataset artifacts and shortcut learning

A second line of work explains why cross-dataset numbers can mislead. Arp et al. catalogue pitfalls in machine learning for security, including spurious correlations and inappropriate baselines (Arp et al., 2022)<!--ref:arp2022dos--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->, and Geirhos et al. describe shortcut learning: decision rules that work on benchmarks but not under shift (Geirhos et al., 2020)<!--ref:geirhos2020shortcut--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->. Audits of CICIDS2017 find flaws in traffic generation, flow construction and labelling (Engelen et al., 2021<!--ref:engelen2021troubleshooting--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->; Lanvin et al., 2023<!--ref:lanvin2023errors--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->), Flood et al. identify six recurring data-design smells across seven benchmarks (Flood et al., 2024)<!--ref:flood2024bad--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->, and a review of 89 public NIDS datasets documents common data limitations (Goldschmidt & Chudá, 2025)<!--ref:goldschmidt2025network--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->. Roy and Minu show that adding raw timestamps lifts tree models on an Internet of Things (IoT) benchmark from about 92.6% to 99.28% macro-F1 under capture-disjoint splits (Roy & Minu, 2026)<!--ref:roy2026unmasking--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->, and Apruzzese et al. argue that research evaluations neglect deployment conditions (Apruzzese et al., 2023)<!--ref:apruzzese2023sok--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->.

The time-to-live (TTL) features of UNSW-NB15 are the best-documented single artifact. Sarhan et al. find that a single TTL-based feature reaches close to the performance of the full feature set, attribute this to the placement of attacker and victim nodes in the testbed, and describe it as similar to "a 'hidden label'" (Sarhan et al., 2021)<!--ref:sarhan2021featureanalysis--><!--anchor:section:5.1.1-->. The same authors drop TTL features from UNSW-NB15 and NF-UNSW-NB15-v2 because of their extreme correlation with the labels (Sarhan et al., 2022)<!--ref:sarhan2022standard--><!--anchor:section:4-->, and Atif and Ali describe TTL in NF-UNSW-NB15-v2 as encoding which machine generated the traffic (Atif & Ali, 2026)<!--ref:atif2026threeaxis--><!--anchor:section:3.3-->. The NetFlow v3 release keeps MIN_TTL and MAX_TTL as ordinary features (Luay et al., 2025)<!--ref:luay2025temporal--><!--anchor:section:Table%201-->; within the sources we screened, none tests whether the artifact persists in v3.

### 2.4 Transfer learning, negative transfer and covariate shift

Transfer learning can fail: when domains are too dissimilar, source data can lower target performance, which is called negative transfer (Pan & Yang, 2010<!--ref:pan2010survey--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->; Zhang et al., 2023<!--ref:zhang2023negative--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->). Ben-David et al. bound the target error of a classifier by its source error plus a divergence between the domains that can be estimated from unlabelled data, and analyse how to weight a small labelled target set against a large source set (Ben-David et al., 2010)<!--ref:bendavid2010theory--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->. When only the input distribution shifts, weighting source examples by the density ratio of target to source inputs corrects the training objective (Shimodaira, 2000<!--ref:shimodaira2000improving--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->; Sugiyama et al., 2007<!--ref:sugiyama2007covariate--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->), and domain-adversarial training learns features that a domain classifier cannot tell apart (Ganin et al., 2016)<!--ref:ganin2016domain--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->. These results supply two of our tools: importance weighting as an adaptation method, and a domain classifier as a measure of how far apart two networks are.
