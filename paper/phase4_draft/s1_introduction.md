# Is It Transfer? A Target-Only Control Finds No Consistent Source Benefit in Few-Shot Cross-Dataset Intrusion Detection on NetFlow

**Rijan Rayamajhi**

## 1. Introduction

Machine-learning (ML) network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) routinely report near-perfect accuracy on the dataset they were trained on, yet they fail when moved to a different network. Cross-dataset evaluations across the major public benchmarks show that models trained on one dataset detect attacks in another at rates far below their in-dataset performance (Cantone et al., 2024)<!--ref:cantone2024cross--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->, and this gap persists across model families, feature sets and benchmark pairs (Apruzzese et al., 2022<!--ref:apruzzese2022cross--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->; D'hooge et al., 2020<!--ref:dhooge2020inter--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->; Layeghy & Portmann, 2023<!--ref:layeghy2023explainable--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->). The difficulty is not new. Sommer and Paxson argued more than a decade ago that intrusion detection differs from other machine-learning domains precisely because operational traffic is diverse and the cost of errors is asymmetric (Sommer & Paxson, 2010)<!--ref:sommer2010outside--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->.

A practical remedy has emerged in recent work: when a detector is deployed on a new network, label a small amount of the new network's traffic and adapt the existing model to it. TAN-IDS, a deployment-oriented evaluation framework for flow-based detectors, reports that fine-tuning a source-trained model on 5% of labelled target data restores random-forest and XGBoost F1 to about 0.98 after zero-shot transfer collapses (Ha Thanh, 2026)<!--ref:hathanh2026tanids--><!--anchor:section:Table%2010-->. Hakim et al. report partial, architecture-dependent recovery for lightweight Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) detectors adapted with limited target data (Hakim et al., 2026)<!--ref:hakim2026crossdomain--><!--anchor:section:5.6-->, and a recent systematic review counts 21 few-shot NIDS studies since 2022 (Roszeitis et al., 2026)<!--ref:roszeitis2026fewshot--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->.

These results share an untested assumption: that the *source* dataset contributes to the recovered performance. Neither TAN-IDS nor Hakim et al. trains a model on the same small set of target labels alone. TAN-IDS compares its fine-tuned models only with zero-shot transfer and with training on the full target split, in a single run without significance tests (Ha Thanh, 2026)<!--ref:hathanh2026tanids--><!--anchor:section:6.2-->, and Hakim et al. note the missing small-sample-only condition themselves (Hakim et al., 2026)<!--ref:hakim2026crossdomain--><!--anchor:section:6.3-->. Without that control, recovery that the target labels would have produced on their own is credited to transfer. Machine learning for security is prone to exactly this kind of pitfall, in which an inappropriate baseline or a spurious correlation makes a model look better than it is (Arp et al., 2022)<!--ref:arp2022dos--><!--anchor:section:abstract-->.

We ask: *when a detector moves to a new network and k target flows per class are labelled, does the source dataset add anything over training on those k flows alone, and why or why not?* We study the four NetFlow v3 datasets (NF-CICIDS2018-v3, NF-UNSW-NB15-v3, NF-ToN-IoT-v3 and NF-BoT-IoT-v3), which share one standardized feature set (Luay et al., 2025)<!--ref:luay2025temporal--><!--anchor:section:Table%201-->, so that all 12 directed dataset pairs can be evaluated under one protocol. Every adaptation method is compared with a model trained on exactly the same target flows and evaluated on the same held-out test set, so any difference can only come from the source, and every comparison is repeated over 10 seeds with paired tests.

Our contributions are:

1. **A leak-free, multi-seed protocol with a target-only control.** Normalization uses only data visible at training time, few-shot samples are nested across label budgets, and every adaptation method is compared with a model trained on the same k target flows (Section 3).
2. **No consistent benefit from the source, for the adaptation methods tested.** Across 12 pairs, three model families and three ways of using the source (pooling, importance weighting and fine-tuning), the source significantly helps in 41 of 540 paired comparisons (8%) and hurts in 205 (38%), uncorrected. Averaged over pairs, it lowers F1 by 0.044 (95% confidence interval (CI) −0.066 to −0.022), and the mean is negative for every source and every target dataset (Section 4).
3. **A non-reproduction and a budget check.** A published zero-shot F1 of 0.81 between UNSW-NB15 and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 does not reproduce on NetFlow v3, and at TAN-IDS's 5% label budget, target-only training matches source-based adaptation (Section 4).
4. **A mechanism consistent with the results.** A domain classifier separates every pair of networks with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of at least 0.996 on raw features, including benign traffic and flows of the same attack category, and 23–40 of the 45 features must be removed before this falls below 0.9. Removing the documented TTL artifact does not change the conclusion (Section 5).
5. **A preprocessing pitfall and a recommended protocol.** Per-domain feature scaling, a common default, shifts categorical codes to dataset-specific values and corrupts separability measurements (Section 5.4); Section 7 recommends practices for cross-dataset NIDS studies.

Section 2 positions these contributions against prior work.
